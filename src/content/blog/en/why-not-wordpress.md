WordPress powers a large part of the web and it has its merits. For most of the small and mid-sized businesses we work with, though, we choose something else. Not because it's trendy, but for three very practical reasons.

## 1. Speed

A typical WordPress site loads a theme, a page builder and dozens of plugins, each with its own code. The result: heavy pages and poor PageSpeed scores.

We generate static pages, optimised at build time and served from a global CDN. On mobile the difference is obvious from the first second, and Google rewards it in rankings.

## 2. Security

Most WordPress breaches come from outdated plugins. A static site has no exposed database, no admin panel to attack and no vulnerable plugins. Fewer moving parts means fewer open doors.

## 3. The real cost over time

A "cheap" WordPress site gets expensive along the way: updates, premium plugin licences, beefier hosting, fixes after an update breaks something. A modern site needs far less maintenance.

## Can I still edit my own content?

Yes. For a blog, news or products we connect a simple CMS with a visual editor. For everything else, we make the changes quickly as part of maintenance.

## When does WordPress still make sense?

If you already have an ecosystem built on WordPress, a team that knows it well, or very specific needs covered by a particular plugin, it may be the right choice. We'll tell you honestly in our first conversation.

> In short: we pick the technology that makes your business faster, safer and easier to grow, not the one we're used to.
